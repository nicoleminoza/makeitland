Favicon review

The poppy cannot be your browser tab icon

Prepared for Leslie Roulias · Site build · July 2026

A favicon is the small square beside a page name in a browser tab. It is 16 pixels wide, about the size of this letter O. Your poppy is drawn with a stem, leaves, and fine roots, and at that size those lines are thinner than a single pixel, so the flower turns into a smudge. I tried five versions in a real tab before recommending one.

Every version below is a full navy square, so they all hold the same weight in a tab. The only difference is whether the shape inside can be recognized.

Both poppy versions lose. The coral one goes soft and reads as a red smudge. The cream one is worse, and that surprised me: the gaps between the petals are transparent in your logo file, so the navy shows through as random creases and it reads like crumpled paper rather than a flower.

Recommendation

Use the C from your own logo

Cut directly from the C in "Camino" in your horizontal logo, in cream on navy. Not a letter typed in some other font, the actual letterform from your logo, so the tab icon and the logo match.

Recommended. C is for Camino, the word people actually use when they talk about you. The navy square does most of the work of being recognized in a row of tabs, and the letter makes it yours. Below, the same C enlarged so you can see the shape is a real C and not a ring.

One small honesty note. At 16 pixels the C as cut is a touch thin, so I thickened it by about half a pixel. This is normal practice for tiny sizes and it is still your letter, but if you would rather ship it untouched, say so and I will.

What does not change

The poppy is not going anywhere. It only steps aside at the two smallest sizes.

  • Your full poppy is still the icon everywhere it can be seen properly: phone home screens, social media avatars, and anywhere 48 pixels or larger
  • The logo itself is untouched
  • No new colors. Navy and cream are already the pair you use everywhere on the site
