Brand rules

The written rules, so the site stays itself

Prepared for Leslie Roulias · Site build · July 2026

Everything on your site follows a small set of rules: five colors doing specific jobs, two typefaces, one corner radius, almost no shadows. Written down, those rules let anyone add a page later without the site drifting. Most of what follows is a summary of what already exists. Two rules need your sign off, because each one closes a door on future choices.

Your colors

Five colors, each with one job

The jobs matter more than the colors. When a color starts doing a second job, the site stops feeling deliberate.

Navy

Headings, main buttons, dark bands, footer

Cyan

Links and prices. Things that are clickable or live

Coral

Accent only. Small marks, never a block of text

Pale blue

Alternating bands, card edges, small badges

Cream

The page itself, and all text on navy

Ink

Body text. Softer than black so cream never glares

Roughly seven tenths of any page is cream and white, two tenths navy and pale blue, and the last tenth is cyan and coral together. If a page starts to look colorful, the accents have grown too far.

01

Coral stays an accent, and never carries text

Coral is the warmest thing in your palette, which makes it tempting to use for headings or a whole section background. It is also a mid-tone, which means text set in it is hard to read for a lot of people, no matter what it sits on. The contrast page covers three specific places this already happens.

What coral is for

15+

Years in finance

A quote, marked with a coral rule.

Numbers, rules, badges, the poppy
What it is not for

A heading in coral

Or a paragraph on a coral background, which is harder to read than it looks and pulls the whole page toward decoration.

Text, backgrounds for text

Recommended. This is the rule that keeps the site feeling calm and professional rather than promotional. It is also the rule most likely to be broken later by accident, which is why it is worth agreeing to in writing now.

02

Photography: real people, daylight, no stock finance imagery

You have two photographs so far. As the site grows it will want more, and this is the rule that decides what fits: photographs of you and of real clients, warm daylight, no filters, no drop shadows. No handshakes, no rising charts, no piggy banks, no laptops beside a coffee cup.

The standard set by what you already have

Square or four by five, gentle corner rounding, no shadow, cropped above the waist so the face reads at any size.

Recommended. Stock finance photography is the fastest way to make a practice like yours look like every firm you are deliberately not. This rule is also a budget question, because it means real photography rather than a subscription library.

The rest, already in place

These are settled and need nothing from you. They are written down so the site stays consistent.

  • Two typefaces on the site. A warm serif for headings and quotes, a clean geometric sans for everything functional: navigation, buttons, prices, body text. Your branding document specifies Futura, which is a print typeface. The sans on the site is its closest web equivalent, and the serif is an addition I made for warmth. Say the word if you would rather the site stay Futura throughout
  • One corner radius on cards and photos, and fully rounded buttons. Nothing else
  • Cards use a thin border rather than a shadow. There is exactly one shadow on the whole site, behind your portrait
  • Sections alternate cream, pale blue, and navy, and two colored bands never sit next to each other
  • Voice: second person, plain words, credentials stated once. No urgency, no hustle language, no shame about money decisions. No exclamation points and no dashes used as pauses
