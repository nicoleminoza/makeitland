Copy changes

Four lines I rewrote after Friday

Prepared for Leslie Roulias · Site build · July 2026

These are the copy edits from our call, already made on the mockups. Two are on the home page and two are on the about page. Each one shows what the line said before, what it says now, and the reason for the change. They are on this page so you can confirm the words are yours before the site goes live, not because anything is unresolved.

01

Your fifteen years, said specifically

Home page, the "Meet your mentor" band, the line directly under the heading.

Before

Fifteen years in finance. Now for you.

True, but it could describe a teller
After

Fifteen years managing global portfolios and risk. Now for you.

Names the actual work

You said on the call that "in finance" undersold it. It does. Fifteen years in finance is a category, and portfolios and risk is a job that not many people can claim. The second half of the line is unchanged, so the turn toward the reader still lands the same way.

02

Compounding as a reason to start, not a warning

Home page, the "Recent grads and career starters" card, last sentence.

Before

Cover letters, offers, and your very first money story. Start here, before the rest of it compounds.

Compounding sounds like a threat
After

Cover letters, offers, and your very first money story. Start now, so it compounds in your favor.

Compounding works for her

"Before the rest of it compounds" implies she is already behind, which is the opposite of how you talk to a twenty three year old. Same financial idea, same length, but the reader is now early rather than late. This matters more than it looks: it is the first sentence a recent grad reads about herself on your site.

Your microfinance training, stated accurately

About page, "The path here," the sentence about Dr. Yunus.

Before

She studied microfinance under Nobel laureate Dr. Muhammad Yunus, then put it into practice with Kiva and Opportunity Fund.

Reads as a direct mentorship

Accurate, and still substantial

04

The crises sentence gets a payoff

About page, "The path here," the sentence immediately before the one above.

Before

Before My Camino, Leslie earned her MBA in International Finance, then managed cash, investments, and foreign exchange for Fortune 500 companies at Bank of America, Oracle, and Equinix, work that took her through some of the biggest financial crises of the last three decades.

Ends on the crises, not on her
After

Before My Camino, Leslie earned her MBA in International Finance, then managed cash, investments, and foreign exchange for Fortune 500 companies at Bank of America, Oracle, and Equinix, work that carried her through some of the biggest financial crises of the last three decades, and left her trusted with more responsibility, not less.

Ends on what it earned her

Two changes. "Took her through" is passive, as if the crises happened to you; "carried her through" gives you the verb. Then the sentence used to stop at the crises, which left the reader thinking about 2008 instead of about you. The new ending is the point of the whole paragraph: surviving three crashes is common, and being handed more after each one is not.

What I did not touch

Your voice is the strongest thing you brought to this project, so the rule I worked to was to change a line only when it was inaccurate, passive, or working against the reader.
