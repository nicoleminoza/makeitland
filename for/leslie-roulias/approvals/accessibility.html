Contrast review

Five small contrast fixes, all inside your palette

Prepared for Leslie Roulias · Site build · July 2026

An accessibility check of the new site measured how strongly each piece of text stands out from its background. The standard tested against, WCAG AA, asks for a ratio of at least 4.5 to 1 on regular text and 3 to 1 on large text, so that people with low vision, older eyes, or a phone in sunlight can read comfortably. Most of the site passes with room to spare. Five spots fall short. Each fix keeps your colors doing the jobs they already do, and they come to you as one approval at the end rather than five separate questions, because these are corrections rather than design choices. Nothing changes until you approve them.

01

Text on navy sections: coral becomes cream

Applies to the small uppercase labels, the underlined links, and the “the money therapist” emphasis inside navy sections on the Home and About pages. Coral at this size on navy is hard to read for many visitors.

04

Small cyan links: one shade deeper

Your cyan works well at large sizes, and the package prices keep it unchanged. At small sizes, the founder credit line on About, the Community Circles link, and the current page marker in the navigation, it falls just short. The proposal deepens cyan only where it is used as small text: #099AAC becomes #067A8B.
