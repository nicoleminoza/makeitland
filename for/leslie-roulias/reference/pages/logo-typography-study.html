Logo study

Keep the typeface. Fix the spacing and the small sizes.

The geometric sans against the site's serif is the strongest decision in this identity. Nothing below changes it. Two problems are worth fixing, one is a nitpick, and one popular recommendation is not worth the money. Every measurement below was read from the pixels of logo-horizontal.png at 500x219 and logo-icon.png at 200x288.

Fix 01, worth doing

Tighten the descriptor

At 0.46em the gaps between letters measure 27.7px, wider than any letter in the word: the widest, A, is 22px and the I is 4px. When the space between forms beats the forms themselves, the eye groups on the gaps and FINANCIAL reads as nine separate letters. Half the tracking still reads as a deliberate spaced descriptor and holds together as a word.

Fix 02, the one that matters

Give the mark a small size version

This is the real cost, and it is not a typography problem. The poppy is drawn with a stem, leaves, and root filaments, all rendered as hairlines. The stem runs 4px wide in the 200px icon and the root filaments 2px, so at a 32px favicon the stem is 0.6px. Below roughly 48px those strokes fall under a pixel and the silhouette stops being a flower. Cropping to the bloom gives a small size mark with no redraw and no loss of recognition.

Current icon, scaled down

By 44px the roots are grey haze. At favicon size the mark is a smudge that could be anything.

Full poppy at 48px and up. Bloom only below. Same flower, cropped. Files are already in this project.

Fix 03, a nitpick

The two lines sit on different left edges

The descriptor is flush right with the name and 5px short on the left, because it was scaled to match the name's width rather than set to a shared axis. Five pixels in a 500px export is one percent of the lockup, visible on a billboard and nowhere else. Correct it the next time the file is open. Do not commission it.

Not recommended

Resetting the wordmark in a new typeface

The obvious suggestion is to replace what looks like a system geometric sans with a properly drawn one. Here is that option, honestly presented, and the reason to decline it.

The difference lives in the M apex and the bowl of the a, at sizes almost no client will see. Against that: every export, every social template, and every printed piece has to be regenerated, and the lockup's proportions shift enough to need re-approval. Spend the round on the small size mark instead. If the logo file is ever reopened for other reasons, reset it then.

One thing outside typography

The wordmark's teal is not a brand token

The logo's teal reads close to cyan teal, the color the guidelines reserve for links and prices. The logo predates the token system and should be exempt from it, not corrected to match it. Add that exemption to the guidelines so nobody recolors the mark to navy for consistency.

Recommended order

  1. Adopt the bloom mark below 48px. The files exist. This fixes the only problem a client actually sees.
  2. Tighten the descriptor to 0.24em. One spacing change to existing artwork, biggest legibility gain per hour.
  3. Exempt the logo from the color tokens in the brand guidelines, in writing.
  4. Align the two left edges only when the file is already open for something else.
  5. Leave the typeface alone. The geometric sans against Newsreader is the identity's best decision. Changing category, weight, or face would cost recognition and buy nothing.
